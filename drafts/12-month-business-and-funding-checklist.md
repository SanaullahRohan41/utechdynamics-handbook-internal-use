The three things I would prioritize above everything else
Given your current UTech situation, I would not spend the next few months chasing certificates.
I would prioritize:
1. Separate business finances
UTech bank account + bookkeeping + invoices.
This starts building financial history immediately.
2. Build project evidence
Every real project becomes:
customer + problem + solution + price + payment + result + evidence
This eventually becomes your strongest asset for procurement and financing.
3. Establish repeatable sales
This is the missing piece that capital cannot solve for you.
You want to eventually be able to say:
"When UTech spends ৳X on sales and development, we can reasonably expect approximately Y business activity."
That's the beginning of an actual business model.

One thing I would not do yet
I would not immediately convert UTech into a private limited company solely for future funding.
There are funding/equity programmes that specifically require a private limited company—for example, Bangladesh Bank's EEF eligibility page describes software projects under that facility as requiring a private limited company and sets specific project-cost conditions. (BB)
But that doesn't mean you should incorporate today.
It means:
When a specific opportunity makes incorporation commercially worthwhile, you'll know why you're doing it.
That's consistent with your broader philosophy.

The UTech principle for the next year
I would put this at the top of your internal handbook:
Build the business before building the funding story.
And the practical version:
Legal compliance → clean banking → accurate accounting → real customers → documented projects → procurement readiness → demonstrated business model → targeted capital.
Not:
Funding opportunity → application → money → figure out what to do with it.
Your current low-overhead setup is actually well suited to this. The objective for the next 12 months is not to make UTech look like a large company. It is to accumulate enough evidence that UTech is a real, functioning business.
That evidence will simultaneously make you more credible to customers, banks, government procurement, grant programmes, private funders, and your own family/friends—without requiring you to take anyone's money prematurely.
